%% ── Abstract ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Semi-fixed menstruation (\textit{veset hatzi kavu'a}) is a central construct in
Jewish Halachic law governing pre-menstrual abstinence. A woman enters a semi-fixed
state when two consecutive menstrual cycles fall within a consistent range of lengths;
once in this state she is obligated to observe pre-menstrual abstinence on the expected
day of onset unless her range excludes the liturgically significant days (day~30 and
the monthly Halachic date). Despite its legal importance, the long-run probabilistic
behaviour of semi-fixed menstruation has not previously been studied formally.

We model the longitudinal dynamics of menstrual cycle lengths as a discrete-time,
finite Markov chain with nine states, partitioned according to whether a cycle is the
first, second, or third-or-later consecutive cycle within a semi-fixed stretch, and by
whether the accumulated range is \emph{short} (maximum $<29$~days), \emph{normal}
($29 \le L \le 31$~days), or \emph{long} (minimum $>31$~days). States $S_{31}$ and
$S_{33}$ are the \emph{favourable} semi-fixed states in which no abstinence at day~30
or at the monthly Halachic date is required.

Using a Natural Family Planning (NFP) dataset of 1{,}554 menstrual cycles from 118
clients (reduced to 1{,}221 cycles from 98 clients after excluding participants who
had established a \haflaga{} fixed menstruation), we estimate the empirical transition
matrix and derive the unique stationary distribution analytically via eigenvalue
decomposition. The stationary probability of the two favourable states is
$\pi_{S_{31}} + \pi_{S_{33}} \approx 8.94\%$, while the overall long-run probability
of being in any semi-fixed state is approximately $63.19\%$.

We further stratify clients into three groups based on their highest-attained
semi-fixed sub-type and show, using Kruskal-Wallis tests followed by
Bonferroni-corrected Dunn post-hoc comparisons, that the three groups differ
significantly in their cycle-length distributions ($H = 386.03$,
$p < 10^{-84}$). Group-specific sub-chain analyses reveal that $S_{31}$-prone
clients reach their favourable state in $18.6\%$ of long-run cycles, while
$S_{33}$-prone clients do so in $17.1\%$, both substantially above the
population-average $8.94\%$. These results provide a rigorous probabilistic
foundation for Halachic guidance and personalised cycle-based prediction tools.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Markov chain; stationary distribution; menstrual cycle;
Halachic law; semi-fixed menstruation; natural family planning; applied probability;
population heterogeneity
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The menstrual cycle is a complex biological process governed by the
hypothalamic--pituitary--ovarian axis, with cycle lengths that vary both within
and between individuals \citep{Treloar1967, Chiazze1968, Munster1992}.
Accurate characterisation of this variability has applications in reproductive medicine,
natural family planning (NFP), fertility treatment scheduling, and—in the context of
the present work—Jewish religious law (Halacha).

In Halachic law, the laws of \textit{Nidah} define periods of obligatory spousal
separation following menstruation and govern the anticipation of the next onset.
A central concept is the \emph{fixed menstruation} (\textit{veset kavu'a}): a
pattern of onset sufficiently regular to impose anticipatory abstinence obligations.
The literature distinguishes several types of fixed menstruation, including the
\haflaga{} (three consecutive equal-length cycles), the \dilug{} (arithmetic
progression of cycle lengths), and the weekly (\textit{hashavua}) patterns
\citep{Ross2023stat, Ross2024reliability}.

Among these, \emph{semi-fixed menstruation} (\textit{veset hatzi kavu'a})—triggered
after two consecutive cycles whose lengths fall within a shared range—has received
the least quantitative attention despite being, as our data confirm, the predominant
long-run state. A woman in a semi-fixed state may be obligated to observe abstinence
on day~30 of the cycle (counting from the most recent onset) and on the monthly
Halachic date, unless her accumulated cycle-length range entirely excludes those days.
This makes the sub-type of semi-fixed state she occupies legally and practically
significant.

\subsection*{Contributions and paper organisation}

This paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We introduce a nine-state Markov chain that fully captures the
        longitudinal semi-fixed menstruation dynamics, with a principled
        state-space that encodes both the ordinal position within a stretch
        and the range sub-type (Definition~\ref{def:states}).
  \item We estimate the empirical transition matrix from a real NFP dataset,
        derive the unique stationary distribution analytically (Proposition~\ref{prop:stationary}),
        and validate it by Monte Carlo simulation.
  \item We show that the long-run probability of being in a \emph{favourable}
        semi-fixed state (no extra Halachic obligations) is $\approx 8.94\%$,
        while that of being in \emph{any} semi-fixed state is $\approx 63.19\%$.
  \item We identify significant population heterogeneity across three client
        sub-groups and compute group-specific sub-chain stationary distributions
        that support personalised Halachic guidance.
\end{enumerate}

The paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:background} provides the
necessary Halachic and mathematical background. Section~\ref{sec:data} describes
the dataset and pre-processing. Section~\ref{sec:model} defines the nine-state
Markov chain and the state assignment algorithm. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents
the transition matrix, stationary distribution, and hypothesis test results.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the implications and limitations.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Halachic Framework}
\label{sec:halacha}

The Halachic interval (\haflaga) between two consecutive menstrual onsets equals
the calendar cycle length plus one, because Jewish law counts the day of onset as
the first day of the new cycle rather than the last day of the preceding one.
Throughout this paper we use the Halachic convention: if the calendar cycle length
(from the end of menstrual flow onset to the next onset) is $L$~days, the Halachic
\haflaga{} is $\tilde{L} = L + 1$.

\begin{definition}[Semi-fixed menstruation]\label{def:semi-fixed}
A woman establishes a semi-fixed menstruation (\textit{veset hatzi kavu'a}) when
two consecutive Halachic cycle lengths $\tilde{L}_{n-1}$ and $\tilde{L}_{n}$ satisfy
$[\min(\tilde{L}_{n-1},\tilde{L}_{n}),\, \max(\tilde{L}_{n-1},\tilde{L}_{n})]
\subseteq \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$. The range $[r_{\min}, r_{\max}]$ defines her semi-fixed
interval. She remains semi-fixed as long as each subsequent cycle length falls within
this interval, which can only widen (absorb a new extreme) or be exited entirely (if
the new length falls outside).
\end{definition}

Once semi-fixed, she must observe anticipatory abstinence on day~30 of the current
cycle and on the day of the month corresponding to her last onset, unless both of
these days fall strictly outside her semi-fixed interval. This motivates the distinction
between sub-types:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Short range} ($r_{\max} < 29$): day~30 and the monthly date both
        fall outside the interval — \emph{no extra abstinence required}.
  \item \textbf{Normal range} ($r_{\min} \le 30 \le r_{\max}$ or the interval
        contains the monthly date): extra abstinence required on at least one day.
  \item \textbf{Long range} ($r_{\min} > 31$): day~30 falls outside the interval,
        relieving that obligation, but the monthly-date obligation may remain.
\end{itemize}

A woman exits the semi-fixed state when a new cycle length falls outside her current
interval; she then restarts the two-cycle acquisition process.

\subsection{Markov Chain Fundamentals}
\label{sec:mc-background}

A discrete-time, finite, homogeneous Markov chain is a stochastic process
$\{X_n\}_{n \ge 1}$ over a state space $\mathcal{S} = \{s_1,\ldots,s_K\}$ satisfying
the Markov property
$\Pr(X_{n+1} = s_j \mid X_n = s_i, X_{n-1},\ldots) = p_{ij}$,
where $\mat{P} = (p_{ij})$ is the $K \times K$ transition matrix with $p_{ij} \ge 0$
and $\sum_j p_{ij} = 1$ for all $i$ \citep{Norris1997}.

If the chain is irreducible and aperiodic, it possesses a unique stationary
distribution $\piv = (\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_K)$ satisfying $\piv \mat{P} = \piv$,
$\pi_k > 0$, $\sum_k \pi_k = 1$ \citep{KemenySnell1960}. In practice, $\piv$ is
computed as the left eigenvector of $\mat{P}$ corresponding to the eigenvalue~1:
\begin{equation}
  \mat{P}^\top \piv = \piv, \qquad \|\piv\|_1 = 1.
  \label{eq:stationary}
\end{equation}

\section{Data and Pre-processing}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Dataset}

We use an anonymised Natural Family Planning (NFP) dataset collected by certified
NFP instructors. Each record corresponds to one menstrual cycle for one participant
and includes the calendar cycle length, cycle number within the participant's
observation period, and demographic variables (age, BMI, parity, etc.).

The raw dataset contains 1{,}665 cycle records from 133 clients. After removing
duplicate (ClientID, CycleNumber) pairs and a small number of records with known
data anomalies (client~8107, cycles 45--53, identified during data collection),
and restricting to participants with at least five recorded cycles to ensure
sufficient longitudinal information, the cleaned dataset comprises \textbf{1{,}554
cycles from 118 clients}, with a mean of $13.2$ cycles per client
(median: 12, range: 5--45).

\subsection{Exclusion of Haflaga-Fixed Participants}

Participants who establish a \haflaga{} fixed menstruation (three consecutive
equal Halachic cycle lengths) operate under a different Halachic regime and
their cycles would introduce structural autocorrelations that violate the intended
semi-fixed dynamics. We therefore exclude the 20 participants who exhibited at
least one \haflaga{} event, yielding the \textbf{analysis dataset of 1{,}221 cycles
from 98 clients} used for all subsequent Markov chain modelling.

\section{The 9-State Markov Chain Model}
\label{sec:model}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{State Space}
\label{sec:states}

\begin{definition}[State space $\mathcal{S}$]\label{def:states}
We define nine states $\mathcal{S} = \{S_{11}, S_{12}, S_{13},
S_{21}, S_{22}, S_{23}, S_{31}, S_{32}, S_{33}\}$ for each cycle, where
the first index encodes the \emph{ordinal position} within the current stretch
and the second index encodes the \emph{range sub-type}:
\begin{align*}
\text{First index:} \quad
  &1 \Rightarrow \text{first cycle of a new stretch (semi-fixed interval resets)},\\
  &2 \Rightarrow \text{second cycle (semi-fixed interval being formed)},\\
  &3 \Rightarrow \text{third or later cycle (semi-fixed state achieved)}.\\[6pt]
\text{Second index:} \quad
  &1 \Rightarrow r_{\max} < 29 \quad (\text{short range}),\\
  &2 \Rightarrow 29 \le r_{\min} \text{ or } r_{\max} \le 31 \quad (\text{normal range}),\\
  &3 \Rightarrow r_{\min} > 31 \quad (\text{long range}).
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

States $S_{3\bullet}$ are the \emph{semi-fixed states}; among them, $S_{31}$ and
$S_{33}$ are the \emph{favourable} states requiring no additional abstinence at
day~30 or at the monthly Halachic date.

\subsection{State Assignment Algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:state} describes the sequential state assignment for a sequence
of Halachic cycle lengths $(\tilde{L}_1, \tilde{L}_2, \ldots)$ belonging to one client.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Semi-fixed state assignment for one participant}
\label{alg:state}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Sequence $(\tilde{L}_1,\ldots,\tilde{L}_N)$, threshold $\theta_{\text{short}}=29$,
         $\theta_{\text{long}}=31$
\Ensure State sequence $(X_1,\ldots,X_N)$
\State $r_{\min} \gets \tilde{L}_1$; $r_{\max} \gets \tilde{L}_1$;
       $\text{sf} \gets 0$ \Comment{$\text{sf}=1$: currently semi-fixed}
\State $X_1 \gets \textsc{SubType}(r_{\min}, r_{\max}, \text{prefix}=\text{S1})$
\For{$i = 2, 3, \ldots, N$}
  \If{$\text{sf}=1$ \textbf{and} $(\tilde{L}_i < r_{\min}$ \textbf{or} $\tilde{L}_i > r_{\max})$}
    \State \Comment{Exit semi-fixed: range broken, restart}
    \State $r_{\min} \gets \tilde{L}_i$; $r_{\max} \gets \tilde{L}_i$; $\text{sf} \gets 0$
    \State $X_i \gets \textsc{SubType}(r_{\min}, r_{\max}, \text{prefix}=\text{S1})$
  \ElsIf{$i=2$ \textbf{or} ($\text{sf}=0$ \textbf{and} $X_{i-2}$ starts with S3)}
    \State \Comment{Second cycle of a new stretch}
    \State $r_{\min} \gets \min(r_{\min}, \tilde{L}_i)$; $r_{\max} \gets \max(r_{\max}, \tilde{L}_i)$; $\text{sf} \gets 0$
    \State $X_i \gets \textsc{SubType}(r_{\min}, r_{\max}, \text{prefix}=\text{S2})$
  \Else
    \State \Comment{Third+ cycle in range, or extending semi-fixed}
    \State $r_{\min} \gets \min(r_{\min}, \tilde{L}_i)$; $r_{\max} \gets \max(r_{\max}, \tilde{L}_i)$; $\text{sf} \gets 1$
    \State $X_i \gets \textsc{SubType}(r_{\min}, r_{\max}, \text{prefix}=\text{S3})$
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\Procedure{SubType}{$r_{\min}, r_{\max}, \text{prefix}$}
  \If{$r_{\max} < \theta_{\text{short}}$} \Return prefix$+$``1''
  \ElsIf{$r_{\min} > \theta_{\text{long}}$} \Return prefix$+$``3''
  \Else\ \Return prefix$+$``2''
  \EndIf
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{remark}
The range $[r_{\min}, r_{\max}]$ is monotonically widening within any stretch: once
a cycle length extends the range, it cannot subsequently shrink. This ensures that the
sub-type index can only transition $1 \to 2$ or $2 \to 3$ (and never backwards) within
a single stretch, which reflects the Halachic principle that an established semi-fixed
interval persists until explicitly broken.
\end{remark}

  \item $S_{1\bullet}$ states transition exclusively to $S_{2\bullet}$ states
        (the sub-type of the destination may differ from that of the origin due
        to the range-widening rule).
  \item $S_{2\bullet}$ states transition exclusively to $S_{3\bullet}$ states.
  \item $S_{3\bullet}$ states either self-loop (with probability 0.43--0.74)
        or transition back to $S_{1\bullet}$ states (range broken).
  \item No state returns directly from $S_{3\bullet}$ to $S_{2\bullet}$, nor
        from $S_{2\bullet}$ to $S_{1\bullet}$.
\end{itemize}

This three-level block structure is fully determined by the Halachic definition
of semi-fixed menstruation and is a \emph{model property}, not a data artefact.

\subsection{Stationary Distribution}
\label{sec:stationary}

\begin{proposition}[Existence and uniqueness of $\piv$]\label{prop:stationary}
The Markov chain with transition matrix $\hat{\mat{P}}$ is irreducible and
aperiodic. Therefore, a unique stationary distribution $\piv$ exists, satisfying
$\piv \hat{\mat{P}} = \piv$ with $\pi_k > 0$ for all $k$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Irreducibility follows by inspection of Table~\ref{tab:transition-matrix}:
every state communicates with every other state within at most four steps.
Aperiodicity follows from the self-loops at $S_{31}$, $S_{32}$, and $S_{33}$
(positive diagonal entries imply period 1).
\end{proof}

We compute $\piv$ as the normalised eigenvector of $\hat{\mat{P}}^\top$ corresponding
to eigenvalue~1. The result is reported in Table~\ref{tab:stationary} and illustrated
in Figure~\ref{fig:stationary}.

\begin{remark}

large self-loop probability ($\hat{p}_{S_{32}, S_{32}} = 0.742$): once a participant
enters the normal-range semi-fixed state, she is likely to remain there for many
consecutive cycles. This persistence is driven by the absorbing tendency of typical
(28--32 day) cycle lengths in the NFP population.
\end{remark}

deviation was $3.6 \times 10^{-4}$, confirming convergence to the analytical
stationary distribution.

\subsection{Sub-Chain Analysis}
\label{sec:subchain}

We re-estimate the transition matrix and stationary distribution separately within
each group's cycle sequences. These \emph{group-conditioned sub-chains} answer the
question: for a woman whose cycle lengths characteristically place her in group
$G_k$, what fraction of her long-run cycles will be spent in a favourable semi-fixed
state?

\subsubsection*{Group $G_{31}$}
The $G_{31}$ sub-chain involves states $\{S_{11}, S_{12}, S_{13},
S_{21}, S_{22}, S_{31}, S_{32}\}$ (state $S_{33}$ never arises in this group by construction).
The stationary probability of the favourable state is
\[
  \pi^{(31)}_{S_{31}} \approx 18.6\%,
\]
more than \emph{four times} the population-level value of $4.1\%$.

\subsubsection*{Group $G_{33}$}
Similarly, the $G_{33}$ sub-chain yields
\[
  \pi^{(33)}_{S_{33}} \approx 17.1\%,
\]
approximately \emph{three and a half times} the population-level value of $4.8\%$.

\subsubsection*{Interpretation}
The population-level stationary distribution $\piv$ provides a global summary, but
substantially underestimates the favourable-state probability for clients with
short or long cycle patterns. Personalised Halachic guidance that leverages a
woman's individual cycle-length profile could therefore predict her semi-fixed
sub-type with far greater accuracy than a population-average model.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Probabilistic Interpretation}

The main finding — that $\approx 63\%$ of cycles lie in some semi-fixed state
in the long run, with $\approx 54\%$ specifically in $S_{32}$ — implies that the
semi-fixed regime is the \emph{default} operating mode of the menstrual cycle in
an NFP-charting population. Far from being a rare special case, semi-fixed
menstruation is the norm. The Halachic obligations it entails (pre-emptive
abstinence on day~30 and on the monthly date) therefore apply to the majority
of cycles for most women.

The finding that only $\approx 8.9\%$ of long-run cycles are in truly favourable
semi-fixed states ($S_{31}$ or $S_{33}$, where no additional obligations apply)
underscores the importance of individual cycle-length profiling: a short-cycle
woman (mean $\tilde{L} \approx 27.6$) has nearly a 1-in-5 chance of being in
the favourable $S_{31}$ state, while the population average suggests only 1-in-11.

\subsection{Comparison with Prior Work}

Prior work by the present author introduced Markov chain models for \haflaga{}
fixed menstruation \citep{Ross2024markov} and permutation tests for fixed-pattern
frequencies \citep{Ross2023stat}. The present model extends this framework to the
more prevalent semi-fixed case, with a richer state space (nine vs.\ two states)
and the additional dimension of sub-type heterogeneity.

Compared to the cycle-length forecasting literature
\citep{Guo2006, Symul2019, Bull2019}, our model is not predictive in the
time-series sense (we do not forecast the next cycle length); rather, it is
characterisational: we describe the long-run occupancy distribution over
Halachically meaningful states. The two approaches are complementary.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Dataset size.} The analysis dataset comprises 98 clients and
        1{,}221 cycles after exclusions. While sufficient for stable transition
        matrix estimation on the major arcs, some transitions (especially those
        entering $S_{11}$ from $S_{33}$ and $S_{23}$ self-loops) have low counts
        and should be interpreted cautiously.

  \item \textbf{Population specificity.} The NFP dataset consists predominantly of
        women who chart their cycles for religious or family-planning purposes;
        this may introduce selection bias relative to the general population.

  \item \textbf{Stationarity assumption.} We assume cycle-length distributions are
        stationary over time within each participant. Systematic within-woman trends
        (due to age, parity, or hormonal changes) are not modelled.

  \item \textbf{Range sub-type boundaries.} The thresholds $\theta_{\text{short}} = 29$
        and $\theta_{\text{long}} = 31$ are determined by Halachic rules; a data-driven
        approach might choose different boundaries and yield a different stationary
        distribution.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

We have introduced a nine-state Markov chain model for the dynamics of semi-fixed
menstruation in Halachic law and derived its unique stationary distribution from
an empirical NFP dataset. The key results are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item In the long run, \textbf{63.2\%} of menstrual cycles lie in a semi-fixed
        state, confirming that this is the dominant mode of the cycle.
  \item Of these, \textbf{8.94\%} are in favourable semi-fixed states ($S_{31}$
        or $S_{33}$) where no additional abstinence obligations arise — the
        remainder ($S_{32}$, $\approx 54.2\%$) carry full Halachic obligations.
  \item Clients stratify naturally into three groups with significantly different
        cycle-length distributions (Kruskal-Wallis $H = 386.03$,
        $p < 10^{-84}$), and group-specific sub-chains show that short-cycle
        ($G_{31}$) and long-cycle ($G_{33}$) women spend $\approx 18.6\%$
        and $\approx 17.1\%$ of their cycles in their respective favourable
        states — far above the population average.
\end{enumerate}

These results provide a rigorous probabilistic foundation for Halachic guidance
on semi-fixed menstruation and demonstrate that personalised, cycle-length-aware
models outperform population-average predictions. Future work will incorporate
Bayesian updating of the transition matrix from an individual's observed cycle
history, enabling real-time personalised predictions.
